=== WRITER NOTE: luna ===
Earlier drafts by you read as "fluent but generic, a polished paraphrase". The judges named three habits. Avoid them:
L1. Generic hedges and boilerplate qualifiers. Do not attach a balancing caveat to claims by reflex ("the net assessment depends on which effect dominates", "the interpretation requires care", "must be weighed against", "does not by itself", "remains an important question"). Use a hedge only where the design calls for one (micro rule 3), at most one per claim, and write a limitation only where the Brief states it, once.
L2. Signposting sentences. Do not write sentences whose job is to introduce the next point or say that something matters ("The distinction between X and Y clarifies what it means to...", "The source of X matters for this balance.", "These events motivate our question.", "This raises the question of..."). Start with the point itself.
L3. Restating results. State each finding once, where it belongs. No interpretive gloss after a result ("These findings suggest...", "Thus, the same X that ... can also ..."), no repeat of the main result in the mechanism, implication or contribution paragraphs; there, refer to it in a few words and add only what is new.
=== WRITER NOTE: ds ===
Earlier drafts by you read as "checklist structure, slogan sentences, telegraphic". The judges named four habits. Avoid them:
D1. List-like paragraphs. Do not build a paragraph from parallel items (channels, counterforces, firms, tests) strung together with semicolons or one-item-per-sentence runs. Write a paragraph as an argument: each sentence follows from the one before (because, so, which means, but), and a set of items is introduced through the logic that links them.
D2. Slogan sentences. No short, catchy restatements of the mechanism or a finding (shapes such as "X buys Y, and Y buys Z", "speed cuts both ways", "X is costly, but it buys Y"). No chiasmus or aphorism. Say the mechanism once, in a full explanatory sentence.
D3. Telegraphic style. Write full, connected prose with articles, verbs and connectives. No noun-stack sentences (shapes such as "A and B are the key frictions."), no clipped clauses joined by dashes or semicolons, no string of sentences under 12 words. Most sentences run 18-32 words (micro rule 1).
D4. Self-announcing structure. Never label the structure of the argument before giving it: no "X provides the first force", "We consider two opposing channels", "The first mechanism we examine is...", "Counterforces push the other way.", "The model also delivers...", "The competitive mechanism is...". Give the channel, force or mechanism itself, with its content, as the subject of the sentence.
Also: give each number once.
